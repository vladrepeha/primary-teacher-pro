# Instructional design: flexible, objective-first planning

Use when planning a lesson, sequence, interdisciplinary project, or revising
an existing lesson. Do not impose a prescribed national lesson-plan template.

## 1. Convert topic into learning

Separate a **topic** ("Responsibility") from an **outcome** ("Given a classroom
situation, students name one action they can take and explain whom it helps").
Good outcomes describe observable student performances, not "learn",
"understand", or "become aware" alone.

Write:

- **Target:** What should a student know or do at the end?
- **Evidence:** What student work, response, explanation, or action would show it?
- **Prerequisites:** What must already be secure?
- **Common confusions:** Which errors or plausible wrong answers might appear?
- **Transfer:** Can students apply it to a new example, not just imitate one?

Use curriculum competencies where provided; do not invent official code
numbers, prescribed terms, or standards.

## 2. Choose a teaching pathway by purpose

| Learning situation | Useful moves |
| --- | --- |
| First encounter with a concept | Concrete experience or example → teacher model → child explanation → guided attempt |
| Practice or fluency | Brief recall → short worked example if needed → varied practice → feedback |
| Investigation and discovery | Question → prediction → evidence gathering → explanation → check alternative |
| Applying knowledge | New problem → choice of method → explanation or product → peer comparison |
| Values, conduct, classroom responsibility | Realistic dilemma → identify effects → justify choices → personal next action; avoid moralizing or shaming |
| Review | Retrieve without immediately showing answers → analyze misconceptions → varied application |

These are options, not mandatory stages. A 7-minute classroom activity does
not need to contain a whole lesson's structure.

## 3. Make cognitive effort visible

Use Bloom's revised taxonomy as a tool for selecting appropriate thinking:

- Remember: retrieve vocabulary, facts, steps.
- Understand: explain, sort, compare, paraphrase.
- Apply: use a method on a new but suitable example.
- Analyze: distinguish cause/evidence, detect an error, group by criteria.
- Evaluate: justify a choice using criteria or evidence.
- Create: produce an original solution, text, model, or action plan.

Verb labels alone do not determine depth. A "create" task can be trivial,
and a well-chosen "explain why" can be demanding. Do not force all levels
or require abstract arguments from children who lack prerequisites.

## 4. Support durable learning

- Activate a *small* amount of relevant prior knowledge.
- Chunk genuinely new material; remove unrelated decoration or lengthy
  directions that compete with the learning target.
- Model at least one complete example when children are unfamiliar with the
  skill; include a common non-example when useful.
- Check for understanding during learning, not only at the end.
- Where appropriate, revisit concepts later with low-stakes recall and
  slightly varied tasks. Never assume repeated exposure means retrieval.
- Allow short explanations, partner talk, manipulatives, drawing, and
  writing when each option elicits the intended thinking.

## 5. Subject-specific safeguards

- **Mathematics:** Move between concrete, visual, and symbolic
  representations. Distinguish calculation errors from concept errors.
  Verify number ranges, units, answer uniqueness, and working.
- **Reading and language:** Select age-appropriate text length and
  vocabulary; distinguish decoding from comprehension. Provide literal,
  inferential, and evidence-based questions when appropriate.
- **Science and environment:** Use observable evidence, fair comparisons,
  explicit safety constraints, and correct distinctions between observation,
  inference, and claim.
- **Civic, spiritual or moral education:** Invite reasoned reflection,
  scenarios, consequences, and constructive actions; do not solicit
  disclosures of a child's family conflicts or private experiences.
- **Art and making:** Preserve creative choice but name the skill being
  practiced and the criteria for reflection.
- **Language learning:** Sequence comprehensible input, meaningful
  interaction, and achievable production; support rather than penalize
  multilingual learners for unrelated language demands.

## 6. Review an existing lesson

Run a five-point audit:
1. Does every major activity advance the stated target?
2. Who does the thinking, and for how long?
3. Are the instructions and materials complete enough to teach?
4. What could go wrong and how will the teacher respond?
5. What evidence will show individual rather than only group understanding?

Preserve the teacher's successful choices, their constraints, and supplied
materials; revise only what is needed.
